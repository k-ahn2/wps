DAPPS v0.40 over the air: the same burst, half the connections

An AX.25 monitor trace from G5ALF-3 (DPSTST's DAPPS) during traffic-harness test 5: four posts into each WPS instance at once, 1.1 s apart. DAPPS v0.40 includes #188 (one session per neighbour, a re-check of the queue before hanging up, and the data line and payload in one frame) and #189 (zstd compression with a shared dictionary). These put into effect proposals 1–3 and 10 from #187. The baseline is the v0.39 trace in #187, plus the same four-and-four burst measured in the earlier analysis.

Findings
  • Batching works. G5ALF-3 pushed all four DPSTST posts (sn 62–65) back to back on one connection. It then sent rev and pulled all four MB7NPW posts (sn 560–563) over the same link. With v0.39 that would have been eight connections, each costing SABM/UA, a prompt, rev, a closing prompt and DISC/UA.
  • Each message now costs about 3 s instead of about 8 s. Inside the session, ihave → send → data → ack repeats every 3 s. The data line and its payload go out as one I-frame, as #188 intended.
  • Compression halves the post payload. Each post went as fmt=z1 with len=197 clen=100 or 101, 49% smaller. The 46-byte WPS ack stayed plain, as designed. The ihave header is now 131 bytes, larger than the payload it announces (#187 proposal 7).
  • The whole burst finishes 45% sooner, but the reverse direction waits longer. The last post landed 41.5 s after the first was queued, against 75.5 s for the same burst on v0.39. MB7NPW's posts were queued by 20:40:55. M0AHN-3 couldn't dial, because G5ALF-3's session was open (#178). So they went out only when G5ALF-3 sent rev after its own four pushes, 32–38 s after queueing. Median time from queue to delivery rose from 21 s to 29 s. The caller's direction is now fast and the callee's direction waits for the whole batch.
  • One lost frame cost 16 s. M0AHN-3's send aff373d (for DPSTST sn 64) never reached G5ALF-3. G5ALF-3 polled at 20:41:07 and M0AHN-3's reply confirmed the offer had arrived. The lost send was only resent when M0AHN-3's own retry timer ran out at 20:41:16. Everything behind it moved 16 s later. Without the loss the burst would have finished in about 25 s.
  • A WPS ack needed a second connection. MB7NPW's WPS held its ack for DPSTST/91 for the usual 30 s. It handed the ack to DAPPS at 20:41:26, while M0AHN-3 was still answering the rev. The drain ended at 20:41:34 without it: #188's re-check runs on the dialling side, not during the other side's rev drain. M0AHN-3 dialled back 2 s after the DISC and spent 18 frames and 5 s on a 46-byte ack. It couldn't travel with an outgoing post, because MB7NPW's posts had all gone before the ack was due.
  • Almost half the frames are bare RRs. 45 of the 95 frames are RRs. 41 are bare acknowledgements, and 33 of those are followed within the same second by an I-frame from the same station. Raising BPQ's RESPTIME (T2), #187 proposal 6, would fold most of them into the I-frames.

What next
  1. Capture test 5 again on v0.41. #190 wakes the forwarder when a message is queued, and #191 holds the link open after traffic. The WPS ack's second connection should go: the ack would go over the held link after a pending / rev exchange.
  2. Let the other side's traffic in between pushes. In #191 a pending from the far end is answered with rev when the caller is free. If "free" means after its whole batch, the reverse direction still waits 30 s or more in a burst like this. Answering pending after each pushed message would alternate the two directions. That suits a WPS conversation, where both sides feel the delay.
  3. Re-check the queue at the end of a rev drain. The side answering rev should pick up anything queued during the drain before it sends its prompt, as #188 already does for the dialling side. With the tail set to 0 this is the only thing that would have saved the second connection.
  4. Raise RESPTIME (T2) on both nodes' port 2. 33 bare RRs were followed within the same second by an I-frame from the same station.
  5. Check FRACK on M0AHN-3's port. Recovering one lost frame took 16 s. At 1200 baud a shorter retry timer would get the burst going again sooner, as long as it stays above the longest normal response time.
  6. Next wire-level wins are pipelining and a shorter ihave. Four turnarounds per message now cost most of each 3 s (#187 proposal 4), and the offer header is bigger than the compressed post (#187 proposal 7).
