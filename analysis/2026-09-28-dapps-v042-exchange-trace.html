DAPPS v0.42 exchange: eight posts in about 9 seconds

An AX.25 monitor trace from G5ALF-3 (DPSTST's DAPPS) during traffic-harness test 5: four posts into each WPS instance, 1.1 s apart. DAPPS v0.42.0 replaces the per-message session with a symmetric exchange (#196, #197, #199). It is compared here with the same test on v0.40 (earlier page) and on v0.41.1, where the harness left 2 s between the two instances' first posts. The trace starts on a link that was already open.

Findings
  • One frame per post. Each post goes as a single msg line with its payload, instead of ihave → send → data → ack. DPSTST 119 was queued at 21:24:47.8, on air at 21:24:48 and acknowledged at 21:24:49. On v0.41.1 each post took about 3 s of turnarounds before its payload moved.
  • Both directions share the link. MB7NPW 584 went out at 21:24:51, in the middle of DPSTST's four posts, and the two nodes' posts and acks interleave from then on. On every earlier version the called node's traffic waited for the caller's whole batch. On v0.41.1 that wait was 10–13 s even after the called node sent pending.
  • Several frames per transmission. G5ALF-3 sent DPSTST 121 and 122 in one key-up at 21:24:51. M0AHN-3 sent MB7NPW 586 (two frames) and the ack for 121 together at 21:24:56. Each key-up costs TX delay and usually an RR, so fewer key-ups is where most of the time went.
  • WPS acks rode on the posts. MB7NPW 584 and 586 are 220 bytes rather than 198. The 22 extra bytes are exactly ,"acks":{"DPSTST":145}, then 146: WPS attaching its held application ack to an outgoing post. So neither node sent a separate WPS ack message during the burst. On v0.40 and v0.41.1 each WPS ack cost its own exchange, and on v0.40 its own connection.
  • The whole burst took 31 frames, including a DAPPS ack for every post and two frames of AX.25 retry. The same burst took 78 frames on v0.41.1.

Rough edges
  • A lost RR held up one ack by 9 s. At 21:24:57 G5ALF-3 sent the acks for MB7NPW 585 and 586, the second with the poll bit set. M0AHN-3's reply isn't in the trace. G5ALF-3 waited for its retry timer, polled at 21:25:05, got the reply at 21:25:06, and only then sent the ack for 587. The post itself had arrived at 21:24:57, so WPS users didn't see this. It only delayed M0AHN-3 learning that 587 was delivered. It's the same slow AX.25 recovery as in the v0.40 trace, but it now costs a late ack rather than a stalled burst.
  • The msg header is now the biggest thing on air. It is 130 bytes, against 101–104 bytes of compressed post. The two 220-byte posts carrying a WPS ack each went as two I-frames, split partway through the header after 121 bytes. 198-byte posts fitted in one. It's worth checking where that split comes from. A shorter header (#187 proposal 7) would help every message either way.
  • Some bare RRs remain: 11 of the 31 frames, plus 2 from the retry. Several are followed within the same second by an I-frame from the same station, so a longer RESPTIME would still fold some in. They matter far less now that each post is one frame.
Next test
  1. Run test 5 with no gap, starting with the link closed. This trace starts on an open link, so it doesn't show a fresh connection or both nodes dialling at once. On #198, v0.42.0 is said to spot the crossing from BPQ's monitor and start the exchange straight away, with the caller also starting after 10 s of silence. A cold start with simultaneous posts would confirm how long that takes over the air.
  2. Capture a longer quiet spell after the burst. This would show the hold ending and the link closing, and how a WPS ack that comes due with no post to ride on is sent.
