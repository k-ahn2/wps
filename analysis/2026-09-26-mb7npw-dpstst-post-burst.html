MB7NPW ↔ DPSTST post burst: where the time goes

Two activity exports, one per node, joined on the DAPPS message id. The 15 test posts (MB7NPW 56–60, DPSTST 37–46) and their acks are plotted on both nodes' clocks. The idle digests from 06:39 are used to check the clocks against each other.

Findings
  • The clocks agree to within about 1 s. Digest transit times are about the same in both directions (median 8.2 s MB7NPW→DPSTST, 9.8 s back; minimum 5.6 s and 6.1 s). A skew of 0.2–0.8 s is all the data allows, so cross-node timings below are good to ±1 s.
  • The software side is fast. From user posting to DAPPS submit is 0.20–0.35 s, and apply is effectively instant. The notify_local_event wake-up works: posts are no longer waiting for the 5 s outbox tick.
  • A lone message takes 6–9 s. Two 5 s cycles sit in series on that path: DAPPS's forwarding tick on the sending node, then WPS's idle inbox poll on the receiving node. As their timing drifts, the idle digest latency steps up and down by about 5 s (chart 3). During a burst WPS polls every 1 s, which removes most of the second wait. The DAPPS tick remains.
  • In a burst, messages queue up on the link. DPSTST handed 40, 41 and 42 to DAPPS within 1 s, and MB7NPW received them exactly 8.06 s apart. MB7NPW 57–60 arrived about 4–7 s apart. Payload size makes no difference (every post is ~200 bytes), so each message is paying a fixed per-message cost on the link.
  • The link carries one direction at a time. DAPPS will not dial a peer while a session with it is open in either direction, because a second connect would reset the live AX.25 link (DAPPS #178). A queued message waits for the first 5 s forwarder tick after that session ends, so the two nodes' queues take turns instead of interleaving. From 07:48:04 both nodes had 4 posts queued. MB7NPW's 4 posts and an ack reached DPSTST first (07:48:11–07:48:28). DPSTST's 44, 45 and 46 only got through after that (07:48:36, 07:49:01, 07:49:14). That is why DPSTST/46 took 74 s from post to peer.
  • Ack batching works as designed. Every ack went out 30.2–30.7 s after the first event it covers was applied. 15 data events needed 6 acks (MB7NPW sent 4 covering 10 events; DPSTST sent 2 covering 5). Still, each ack takes a link slot, and in the congested minute 07:48 two of them were queued behind or between data.
  • Digest suppression works. No digests after 07:45 in either direction, because acks show both peers level.
  • Both nodes restarted around 07:44. Their digests were off the usual 5-minute cadence. After the restart, MB7NPW received DPSTST's digest (latest 36) and sent an ack for DPSTST/36, via the "level, but no ack from this process" path in _handle_digest. It is harmless, but it is one extra message.

What would help
  1. Batch several events into one DAPPS message. The link costs a fixed ~4–8 s per message whatever the size. If the outbox pump packed everything outstanding (up to a byte cap) into one payload, the 4-post bursts here would each take one slot instead of four. The biggest post-to-peer time would drop from about 74 s to about 10 s. Seq order is kept inside the batch, so apply order is unchanged.
  2. Carry pending acks on outgoing data. At 07:48:03 MB7NPW sent post 57 and, 0.4 s later, a separate ack for DPSTST/42. If held acks rode along on any outgoing data batch, the separate ack message would only be needed when there is no data to send.
  3. Skip the post-restart ack when the digest says the peer's ack is already known. This is minor, one message per restart. Alternatively accept it as the price of robust outbox retirement.
